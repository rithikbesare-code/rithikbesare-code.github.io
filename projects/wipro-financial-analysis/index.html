01

Scope and approach

The workbook contains annual profit and loss, balance-sheet and cash-flow data from March 2015 through March 2024. The report analyses profitability ratios, return on equity, asset use, EPS growth, dividend payout, peer metrics, ESG themes and a dividend discount model.

The workbook does not label its financial statement units. Its FY2024 sales and profit figures match Wipro’s consolidated FY2024 financial statements when read as ₹ crore: the workbook shows ₹89,760.3 crore of revenue and ₹11,045.2 crore of profit, while Wipro reports ₹897,603 million of revenue and ₹110,452 million attributable to equity holders. Operating margin is recalculated from the workbook’s Profit & Loss tab as operating profit divided by sales; it is not reconciled to Wipro’s IFRS operating result. ROE is recalculated as net profit divided by the average of current- and prior-year share capital plus reserves. Amounts are presented in ₹ crore; the workbook omits a unit label, so this scale is inferred from the FY2024 source comparison above.

The workbook’s gross-profit formula subtracts only power and fuel plus employee costs from sales and leaves out several other expense lines. I do not present that output as a standard gross margin.

Interpretation: Operating margin peaked at 23.85% in FY2021 before settling at 18.66% in FY2024. Net margin fell from 15.90% in FY2020 to 12.31% in FY2024. Average-equity ROE peaked at 20.33% in FY2022 and ended at 14.51%, so the five-year pattern is not a steady annual decline.

03

DuPont analysis and formula review

The DuPont method expresses ROE as net profit margin × average asset turnover × average equity multiplier. Recomputing the equity denominator from the workbook’s balance-sheet inputs gives the following FY2020 and FY2024 comparison:

AVERAGE SHAREHOLDERS’ EQUITY

The DuPont tab calculates current-year equity plus half of prior-year equity. The average should be (current-year equity + prior-year equity) ÷ 2. The submitted formula therefore inflates the denominator and produces a lower ROE series, including 11.64% for FY2020 and 9.74% for FY2024.

ROE DEFINITIONS

The Ratio tab instead divides profit by year-end equity, producing a different series. The page uses an explicitly recalculated average-equity measure for the DuPont comparison and identifies its denominator.

PERIOD ALIGNMENT

The Ratio tab’s operating-profit and PBT margin rows link through Data Sheet formulas that point to earlier years than the displayed dates. I recalculated operating margin from the matching annual Profit & Loss columns; the submitted ratio summary should not be read as a correctly aligned five-year series.

04

Peer comparison and risk discussion

The assignment compares Wipro with Infosys, Tata Consultancy Services and HCL Technologies using profitability, return, turnover and dividend measures. It also discusses demand for IT services, currency exposure, interest rates and inflation, competition, automation, regulation and geopolitical conditions.

The peer values are typed into the workbook rather than linked to a dated source schedule. Their reporting periods and ratio definitions are not shown, and some Wipro figures do not match the workbook’s own FY2024 statements. The comparison is therefore useful as a research framework but cannot support a like-for-like ranking without source and period checks.

Risk scope: The report identifies plausible categories of business and market risk. It does not quantify their impact on Wipro’s cash flows, valuation or probability of loss.

05

Dividend discount valuation

The workbook’s single-stage dividend discount calculation uses a FY2024 dividend per share of ₹1, expected growth of 11.6% and a 12% required return. With the next dividend of ₹1.116, the formula (P_0 = D_1 / (r - g)) returns ₹279.00 per share.

TERMINAL GROWTH

The multi-year forecast includes 13% dividend growth while the discount rate is 12%. The terminal-value formula therefore has a negative denominator and returns approximately −₹180.35. A perpetual-growth terminal value requires growth below the discount rate.

PRICE REFERENCES

The workbook includes ₹240.05 as the FY2024 year-end price, ₹317.10 in its current-price field and ₹325.55 in the dividend-yield formula. The files do not provide matching observation dates for these figures.

CONCLUSION

The report recommends selling based on its stated ₹202.91 value. Because that value, the forecast schedule, the formula output and the share-price inputs do not reconcile, the submitted files do not establish a reliable investment conclusion.

06

ESG review and evidence limits

The report covers environmental practices, employee and community topics, governance, data privacy and possible links between sustainability and financial performance. It also proposes stronger supply-chain disclosure and employee-wellbeing coverage.

Several ESG examples do not have traceable, company-specific sources, and one passage attributes the Tata Code of Conduct to Wipro. Some cited titles and organizations appear mismatched to the claims. I have kept the ESG work as a qualitative review topic rather than republishing those statements as verified company facts.

07

Overall assessment

This coursework brings together statement analysis, profitability ratios, peer comparison, DuPont decomposition, dividend valuation, business risks and ESG themes. The strongest reproducible findings are the annual revenue and profit series, the margin trends recalculated from matching statement periods, and the corrected average-equity ROE.

The report’s sell conclusion is not carried forward because the DDM calculations and share-price dates conflict. The peer data and ESG citations also need source validation. This is a historical academic review based on information through FY2024, not current investment advice.
