FINA-1032-M01 · MINI LITERATURE REVIEW

Who holds power when owners don’t control?

A review of the agency costs that can arise when ownership and day-to-day corporate control are separated, and of the governance mechanisms used to monitor and align managerial decisions.

01

Research focus

How can corporate governance reduce the agency costs associated with a separation between ownership and control, and where do the available mechanisms fall short?

The review treats the split between investors who own a company and managers who direct its operations as a governance problem. Managers have decision-making discretion and information that outside owners may not share; governance mechanisms aim to improve oversight, align incentives and make decisions more accountable.

The review’s central theme is that governance works through a combination of monitoring, incentives and shareholder voice. Each mechanism can help, but its design and the information available to decision-makers matter.

02

Mechanisms and tensions

01 / BOARD OVERSIGHT

Board independence

Independent directors can strengthen challenge and oversight. The review also recognises that formal independence alone may not overcome information gaps between management and the board.

02 / INCENTIVE DESIGN

Performance-based pay

Linking reward to results may align managers with investors, while narrow or short measurement periods can encourage short-term decisions. The paper points to a need for balanced measures and longer horizons.

03 / SHAREHOLDER VOICE

Shareholder activism

Investor proposals and voting can add external scrutiny. The review presents activism as a mixed tool whose effectiveness and effects depend on the issue, sponsor and context.

04 / WIDER ACCOUNTABILITY

ESG and stakeholders

Environmental, social and governance concerns broaden the outcomes companies are asked to consider. The coursework also connects this wider accountability question to AI governance and its public-interest implications. The debate is how to include those interests while keeping managerial responsibilities and accountability clear.

03

What the review concludes

The coursework argues for a balanced governance approach: retain monitoring and accountability to shareholders while taking wider stakeholder and sustainability concerns seriously. It treats established tools such as boards, incentives and shareholder engagement as useful, but not sufficient on their own.

01 / OVERSIGHT

Board structures matter most when directors have the information and authority needed to question management.

02 / INCENTIVES

Compensation design should be assessed for the behaviour it rewards, including its time horizon and choice of measures.

03 / ACCOUNTABILITY

Broader stakeholder aims need clear responsibilities and ways to evaluate progress.

Scope: This is a portfolio synopsis of a mini literature review, not an original empirical study. It summarises the themes and position of the submitted coursework; it does not claim to establish causal effects or settle the shareholder-versus-stakeholder debate.
