Portfolio construction & performance

A two-part investment-management study comparing four FTSE 100 equity strategies and a multi-asset fund against its benchmark. The work examines return and risk trade-offs, asset allocation and security selection, then checks the report’s conclusions against the workbook calculations.

01

Equity portfolio strategies

How do short-sale permissions and beta-based selection change the return and risk profile of a 30-share portfolio?

The workbook constructs four FTSE 100 equity portfolios: no short sales, short sales allowed, high beta and low beta. It uses monthly share-price observations to calculate portfolio returns and compares the four approaches with the FTSE 100 over 12 monthly observations from December 2023 to November 2024.

StrategyAnnualised returnAnnualised volatilitySharpe ratio

Returns compound the average monthly return. Volatility annualises monthly standard deviation by √12. The equity-strategy Sharpe ratios use monthly excess return divided by monthly standard deviation.

Short-sale exposure: The short-sale portfolio’s net weights sum to 100%, but its absolute weights total about 442%. Nineteen of the 30 positions are short. This higher gross exposure helps explain why its 17.36% return comes with 17.21% annualised volatility, versus 7.32% for the FTSE 100 reference.

03

Performance attribution

The workbook decomposes the multi-asset portfolio’s average monthly excess return into asset allocation, security selection and interaction effects. The effects reconcile to the total return difference in the attribution schedule.

EffectReturn contributionScaled to £450,000
Asset allocation−0.1336%−£601
Security selection+0.0515%+£232
Interaction−0.0727%−£327
Total relative result−0.1549%−£697

The £ figures scale the average monthly attribution contributions by the workbook’s £450,000 reference amount. They are relative contributions, not a statement that the fund’s closing balance fell to £700.

Cash overweight and the absence of bond holdings contributed to the negative allocation effect. Security selection added a small positive contribution, but it did not offset the allocation and interaction effects.

04

Review of the report and workbook

RISK-RATIO FORMULAS

In the multi-asset section, the workbook subtracts standard deviation, beta and downside deviation from the risk premium to produce Sharpe, Treynor and Sortino labels. Those measures require division. The corresponding reported ratios are therefore not reliable and are not presented here as valid results.

PORTFOLIO VS BENCHMARK

The workbook assigns EEM, BLV and BIL to the portfolio, while IEMG, AGG and IRX form the benchmark. The report sometimes describes all six as holdings in the same portfolio.

£450,000 INTERPRETATION

The roughly £697 figure is the average monthly relative attribution scaled to the workbook’s reference amount. It is not the portfolio’s ending value or total loss.

SHORTING ASSUMPTIONS

The short-sale portfolio has about 442% gross exposure. The workbook does not show explicit borrowing, margin or transaction-cost adjustments alongside the reported returns.

The multi-asset performance comparison uses 12 monthly observations. The report cites broad publisher homepages, but the files do not identify exact market-data providers or price retrieval dates, limiting independent reproduction.
