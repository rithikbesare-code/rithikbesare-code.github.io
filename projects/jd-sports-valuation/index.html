JD Sports Fashion plc valuation

01

Question and scope

What equity value does a forecast-based DCF produce for JD Sports under the coursework assumptions, and which assumptions have the greatest effect on that result?

The submitted work brings together two written analyses and an Excel workbook. It reviews JD Sports’ strategy and operating context, looks across financial statements and ratios, and uses a DCF and peer-company comparison to frame a valuation discussion. The workbook labels its starting sales input as 2024 and forecasts eight periods.

The page reports the workbook’s outputs as coursework results. They are not a current price target, a live market comparison or investment advice.

02

DCF model design

The workbook grows its sales base at a constant rate, applies a fixed operating margin and tax rate, estimates investment in fixed capital and working capital as percentages of incremental sales, then discounts the forecast cash flows. It adds a terminal value after the eighth forecast year and subtracts the debt input before dividing by the diluted share count.

04

Sensitivity and company analysis

The sensitivity sheet varies sales growth, operating margin, tax and the required return. In the submitted tables, increasing sales growth or operating margin raises the modelled shareholder value, while higher tax and a higher required return reduce it.

01 / SALES GROWTH

The workbook’s sales-growth cases run from 12.5% to 18.5%; the displayed shareholder-value results range from about £2,972m to £6,003m.

02 / OPERATING MARGIN

At the base 9.6% required return, the margin table ranges from 1.5% to 4.5% and displays values from about £1,250m to £7,472m.

03 / STRATEGIC CONTEXT

The reports also cover Porter’s Five Forces, SWOT, horizontal and vertical statement analysis, liquidity, profitability, leverage and operating-efficiency ratios.

The peer discussion selects Foot Locker and NEXT as comparators. The submitted peer-multiple discussion contains contradictory figures, so those multiples are not repeated here as verified findings.

05

Model review and limitations

Reviewing the supporting files surfaced issues that affect how the result should be interpreted. They are included here to make the project record transparent and to distinguish the submitted model from a fully checked valuation.

01 / CASH-FLOW SIGN

The DCF inputs enter both incremental investment rates as −5%, producing negative investment line items. The free-cash-flow formula then subtracts those already-negative amounts, which adds them to cash flow. This appears to overstate forecast free cash flow and affects the valuation and sensitivity results.

02 / DISCOUNT RATE

The DCF discounts at 9.6%, while the workbook’s WACC calculation returns about 8.20%. The two rates are not reconciled in the submitted files.

03 / OPERATING MARGIN

The DCF workbook forecasts a 3.0% margin, while the second written report states a 12.5% 2024 operating margin. The documents do not explain or reconcile this difference.

04 / TERMINAL VALUE & PEERS

The terminal value uses a simplified zero-growth perpetuity proxy. The peer section also contains inconsistent valuation multiples. Both areas need source, period and formula checks before they can support a decision.

For these reasons, the £4.36bn equity value and 84.16p per-share result are reproduced from the submitted workbook and are explicitly unvalidated. Correcting the cash-flow signs and reconciling the source assumptions could materially change the result.
