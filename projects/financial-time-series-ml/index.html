01

Data and research design

How much can market returns explain company excess returns, and do past stock returns help describe or forecast the next period?

The panel exercise combines four Indian listed companies from different sectors: HDFC Bank, MRF Limited, Deepak Nitrite and Tata Power. It uses the latest 100 trading days for each company alongside a market index and risk-free rate. Returns are calculated as rₜ = ln(Pₜ / Pₜ₋₁), then arranged as a balanced panel with 400 company-day observations.

A separate time-series exercise selects HDFC Bank. Its EViews outputs show 493 daily observations spanning roughly May 2022 to October 2024. The assignment covers panel estimators, return autocorrelation, ARMA forecasting, ARCH testing, GARCH volatility and a conceptual neural-network use case.

The panel and time-series exercises use different sample lengths. The coursework does not clearly document the raw price, market-index and risk-free data providers or their retrieval dates, which limits exact reproduction.

That means the analysis does not reject a zero intercept; by itself, it does not establish that CAPM is correct or that the model explains returns well.

Model-selection note: The report describes FE and RE as alternatives but does not show a Hausman test or another documented selection rule. It therefore does not establish which panel estimator is preferred.

03

Return dynamics and ARMA forecast

The HDFC Bank correlogram reports 36 lags. ACF and PACF values stay close to zero, and the Ljung–Box test does not show strong evidence of serial correlation in the mean returns; the reported lag-36 p-value is 0.484.

01 / MODEL FIT

The ARMA output reports R-squared 0.0353, adjusted R-squared 0.0234 and Durbin–Watson 1.933. The overall F-test p-value is 0.0074, while most individual lag terms are not statistically significant.

02 / FORECAST SHAPE

The out-of-sample return forecast is nearly flat around zero and misses the larger observed moves. Reported errors include RMSE 0.01386, MAE 0.00858 and symmetric MAPE 182.4%.

03 / SPECIFICATION CHECK

The assignment asks for ARMA(3,2), but the EViews coefficient table labels AR(6), AR(19), AR(30), MA(6) and MA(19). The stated order and displayed output do not match.

The results suggest that a simple conditional-mean model did not capture the sharp return movements in this sample. A low RMSE alone can be misleading when returns are concentrated near zero; the high relative error and flat forecast are relevant diagnostics.

04

Volatility tests and GARCH

The ARCH LM test reports a p-value of 0.0562. This is close to, but above, the conventional 5% threshold; the result is marginal evidence rather than a clear rejection of constant variance at that level.

The EViews variance equation shows a significant lagged squared-shock term but an insignificant lagged-variance term. The forecasted mean remains close to zero, while the forecast variance settles to a nearly constant level and misses several sharp moves.

WRITTEN INTERPRETATION

The report says the GARCH coefficient is 0.7171 and significant, implying strong volatility persistence.

DISPLAYED EViews OUTPUT

The estimated GARCH(−1) coefficient is 0.027329 with p-value 0.7871. The displayed output does not support the written claim of a significant 0.7171 coefficient.

The volatility section is a useful model-checking exercise: its charts and diagnostics show why a model label or a narrative interpretation needs to be checked against the underlying output.

05

Machine-learning application

The final section explains machine learning, supervised learning, training and test sets, and neural-network structure. It proposes a stock-forecasting workflow using historical prices and economic indicators, normalization, a train/test split, an RNN or LSTM, backpropagation and out-of-sample evaluation.

Scope: The document presents a proposed neural-network application; it does not train or evaluate a neural network. The empirical analysis in the assignment uses econometric outputs, including panel regressions, ARMA and GARCH.
