COURSEWORK ESSAY · INTEREST RATE DERIVATIVES

Interest rate swaps:
pricing, hedging & risk.

A structured study of how fixed and floating interest payments are exchanged, how a par swap rate is valued, and how firms and investors use swaps to manage or take interest-rate exposure.

01

Project focus

How do interest rate swaps work, how is their fixed rate determined, and how can they help manage interest-rate exposure?

The essay moves from contract features to valuation and practical use. It reviews fixed-for-floating swaps and floating-for-floating basis swaps, then discusses related products such as cross-currency and inflation swaps. Its practical themes include corporate hedging, portfolio positioning, speculation, asset–liability management and risk control.

A swap exchanges interest cash flows calculated on a notional amount. In a standard single-currency vanilla swap, the notional amount is used to calculate payments; it is not itself exchanged.

02

How the contract works

01 / FIXED–FLOATING

Two payment legs

One party pays fixed and receives floating; the other pays floating and receives fixed. The benchmark, notional, dates, payment frequency and day-count convention determine the cash flows.

02 / BENCHMARKS

Reference rates

The coursework uses LIBOR and SOFR examples. For contemporary USD context, the page uses SOFR-based examples; other currencies have their own benchmarks and conventions.

03 / RATE EXPOSURE

Position direction

In simple terms, paying fixed and receiving floating tends to gain value when market rates rise; receiving fixed and paying floating tends to gain value when rates fall. Actual value also depends on schedule, curve and contract details.

04 / RELATED STRUCTURES

Basis and other swaps

A floating-for-floating basis swap exchanges payments tied to different floating benchmarks. Cross-currency and inflation swaps extend the idea but introduce additional currency or inflation exposures.

03

Valuation in the coursework

The essay explains swap valuation as a comparison of the present value of the two legs. At inception, the par fixed rate is chosen so that the discounted fixed and floating cash flows have equal value under the pricing assumptions and contract conventions.

Simplified cash-flow notation: PV of the fixed leg = N × K × Σ(αᵢ × DFᵢ). PV of the floating leg = N × Σ(αᵢ × Fᵢ × DFᵢ). Here N is the notional, K the fixed rate, αᵢ the period accrual fraction, Fᵢ the projected floating coupon for the period, and DFᵢ its discount factor. For compounded overnight swaps, the floating coupon is built from rates compounded across the accrual period.

Worked illustration

Using the three annual periods shown in the coursework’s worked calculation, the notional is $100 million, the discount factors are 0.99, 0.97 and 0.95, and the floating coupons are 3.0%, 3.3% and 3.5%.

Discounted value: $2.970m + $3.201m + $3.325m = $9.496m.

02 / FIXED LEG AT 3%

Discounted value: $2.970m + $2.910m + $2.850m = $8.730m.

03 / PAR RATE

With annual accrual fractions of 1, the fixed rate that equates the legs is about 3.26%.

The example is deliberately simplified. Real valuations use contractual payment schedules, accrual fractions, benchmark compounding, market curves, collateral terms and currency-specific conventions. Projection curves estimate floating coupons; discount factors value the resulting payments.

04

Applications and risk

01 / HEDGING

Stabilising floating debt

A borrower with floating-rate debt can pay fixed and receive floating in a swap. The received benchmark can offset the floating benchmark on the debt, leaving a more predictable fixed benchmark cost plus any loan spread or basis mismatch.

02 / INVESTMENT

Taking a rate view

Investors may use swaps to express a view on rate changes or the shape of a yield curve. Such positions can gain or lose value as rates move and should not be described as guaranteed arbitrage.

03 / PORTFOLIOS

Duration and balance sheets

Swaps can adjust a portfolio’s interest-rate sensitivity or help an institution manage gaps between asset and liability rate exposures.

04 / RISK CONTROL

Risks to manage

Key risks include rate and curve movements, basis mismatch, counterparty exposure, collateral needs, liquidity, model assumptions and legal or operational terms. A hedge can reduce a targeted exposure without removing every risk.

Benchmark update: The source essay includes legacy LIBOR examples. The U.S. clearing framework has transitioned specified swaps from LIBOR toward overnight reference-rate products such as SOFR. The portfolio synopsis uses SOFR for current USD context and treats benchmark choice as currency- and contract-specific.
