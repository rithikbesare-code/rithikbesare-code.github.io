CHEN–BESARE · MSc FINANCE & INVESTMENT DISSERTATION

Unravelling UK IPO outcomes

This dissertation studies the post-listing performance of UK IPOs and asks whether company characteristics and market conditions help explain longer-run outcomes. I reviewed the dissertation alongside its Excel panel and EViews output, separating the estimates reported in the paper from results that can be checked directly in the supplied workbook.

01

Research question and study design

The dissertation asks how UK IPOs perform after listing and whether size, pre-IPO profitability, leverage, initial underpricing, equity dilution and market conditions help explain their later returns. The report describes a purposive sample of 50 companies, each followed for four annual observations, with the FTSE 100 used as the market benchmark.

The supplied workbook contains 200 firm-year rows across calendar years 2010–2024. The IPO-year field itself ranges from 2010 to 2021. Each company appears four times, so the 2010–2024 span describes the calendar years represented in the panel; it does not mean the workbook contains IPOs issued throughout all 15 years.

01 / OUTCOME

Post-listing performance

The dissertation defines buy-and-hold abnormal return (BHAR) against the FTSE 100 over a multi-year holding period.

02 / EXPLANATORY FACTORS

Company and market data

Reported factors include IPO size, ROA, debt ratio, underpricing, equity dilution, GDP growth, interest rates and market sentiment.

03 / ESTIMATION

Panel random effects

The dissertation reports random-effects GLS models in EViews 12 Student Lite with robust standard errors.

BHAR = compounded IPO-company return − compounded benchmark return

The equation summarises the dissertation’s stated buy-and-hold comparison. The paper identifies the FTSE 100 as the benchmark; the calculation could not be reproduced from the supplied workbook values.

04

Panel-model and interpretation limits

HAUSMAN TESTS

All three EViews screenshots show a Hausman statistic of zero with p = 1.000, but also state that the cross-section test variance is invalid and the statistic was set to zero. Each includes a warning that robust standard errors may not meet the Hausman variance assumptions. These outputs do not provide valid evidence that random effects is preferred. EViews documentation also cautions that Hausman results may be suspect when robust standard errors are used.

MODEL SAMPLE

The workbook has four annual rows per company, but the IPO-level explanatory fields are mostly present only once per firm. The output reports 200 observations. Without the EViews workfile or a documented rule for expanding those variables, it is unclear which observations contributed to each coefficient.

BHAR OUTCOME

The BHAR column is central to the dissertation’s outcome, yet it does not reconcile with the supplied stock and FTSE return series under the stated benchmark comparison. Long-run abnormal-return methods are sensitive to how the holding-period return and reference portfolio are constructed; the workbook does not identify a reproducible calculation.

CONCLUSION VS TABLES

The conclusion describes size and dilution as significant or more consistent drivers, while the displayed tables show their p-values above 0.05. The abstract, results narrative and conclusion also differ on the importance of profitability. The numerical tables are presented here as the reported output, with the conflicting interpretation noted.

The supplied EViews screenshots use a robust covariance option labelled “White period (cross-section cluster)” and report weighted R². These are details of the reported output, not a substitute for a valid model-selection test or an independently reproducible panel.

05

What the project shows

The dissertation brings together IPO characteristics, company accounts, annual market returns and macroeconomic variables in a UK listing study. Its three reported models consistently show a positive ROA association, while size, debt, underpricing, dilution and the macro variables do not reach the 5% significance threshold in the corresponding tables.

Those results should be read as findings reported by the dissertation rather than independently verified estimates. The source workbook’s formula mismatches, incomplete IPO-level fields, unreconciled BHAR values and invalid Hausman warnings prevent a reliable replication from the files supplied.

For a reproducible re-analysis

  1. Document the IPO event date, annual observation windows and the four-year holding-period convention for every firm.
  2. Recalculate IPO characteristics and accounting ratios from traceable inputs, then document how time-invariant variables enter the panel.
  3. Rebuild BHAR from firm and benchmark total-return series with a stated dividend and delisting policy.
  4. Provide the model-ready data and estimation commands, then rerun the model and report a valid specification check.
